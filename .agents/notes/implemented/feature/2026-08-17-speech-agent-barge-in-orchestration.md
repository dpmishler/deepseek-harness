# Agent Note: Barge-in-aware speech-agent orchestration over ctx.speech

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-08-17-speech-agent-barge-in-orchestration.zh.md)

## Problem

`@deepseek-ai/dsh-speech` (the Service Definition) and its Deepgram Flux STT/TTS providers open provider-neutral streaming sessions, but nothing in the repository turned a finalized STT transcript into an LLM request or streamed the LLM's response into TTS. Without that Consumer, `ctx.speech` had no model-facing surface at all, and a caller wanting a voice conversation had no barge-in, cancellation-propagation, or playback-offset handling to build on — every consumer would have had to invent its own turn-taking state machine, generation-tracking, and Flux `Interrupt` offset bookkeeping from scratch. An earlier attempt at this slice (commit `33dbd22972`, reverted at `e59b02d46f`) duplicated the seam itself as a second `ctx.voice` registry alongside `ctx.speech` and shipped with real regressions (a truncated test file, a flush-count assertion mismatch, and provider-dispatch error-contract mismatches).

## Decision

`@deepseek-ai/dsh-speech-agent` is a Consumer package with no `ctx` key of its own: `TurnController` accepts already-open `SttSession`/`TtsSession` values (opened by the caller through `ctx.speech.openStt()`/`openTts()`) and a `respond(transcript, signal): AsyncIterable<StreamChunk>` callback — the same `@deepseek-ai/dsh-llm` chunk vocabulary `LlmRuntime.stream()` already produces, so a real `respond` is a thin closure around `ctx.llm.stream()`. This keeps the package Cordis- and session-log-free by design: `TurnController` is unit-testable against fake sessions with no context at all, and `./consumer.ts` (a separate module, kept out of the main barrel) is the only file that depends on `@deepseek-ai/dsh-session`.

Every response gets a monotonically increasing `generation` number, bumped both on a normal response start and on barge-in. `TurnController` pumps `SttSession.events` and `TtsSession.events` concurrently: a `completed` STT turn starts a new generation and streams the model's `text-delta` chunks straight into `TtsSession.speak()`, flushing on stream end; a `started` STT turn arriving while the controller is `thinking` or `speaking` is barge-in — it aborts the in-flight `respond()` call via `AbortSignal`, halts local playback, and calls `TtsSession.interrupt()`. A `speakingGeneration` field recorded at `turn-started` lets the TTS pump drop `audio` frames whose generation was superseded before they arrived (`TurnController.discardedFrameCount`) instead of handing stale audio to the caller.

`PlaybackClock` owns the session-wide "milliseconds of audio actually heard" counter Flux TTS's `Interrupt.playback_offset` requires. Its `consumeInterruptOffsetMs()` returns `undefined` instead of repeating the previous interrupt's offset — the protocol correctness fix this design adds over the reverted line: Flux TTS answers a non-advancing offset with `INVALID_INTERRUPT_OFFSET` and ignores the interrupt as reconciliation (though the caller's own cancellation, already issued, is unaffected), which the reverted code did not guard against.

`onResponseGenerated(generation, text)` fires exactly once per generation — with the complete text on a normal flush, or with whatever had streamed so far when barge-in superseded the generation before it flushed — giving the "generated" half of generated-vs-heard reconciliation. `onSpeechInterrupted`/`onTurnCompleted` now also receive that same `generation` (read from `speakingGeneration`), so `./consumer.ts`'s `withSessionLogging` can durably log all four `speech-agent/*` events (`transcript`, `response`, `turn-completed`, `turn-interrupted`) joined by one id, without inferring correlation from log position. `./invariant.ts` checks two relationships across a session's whole durable log: `speech-agent/transcript` generations strictly increase, and `speech-agent/turn-interrupted.audioPlayedMs` never decreases (mirroring `PlaybackClock`'s own contract); when both are present, `textSpoken + textRemaining` must reconstruct the matching `speech-agent/response` text verbatim.

## Alternatives considered

**A second `ctx.voice` capability seam alongside `ctx.speech`.** This is exactly what the reverted `33dbd22972` shipped and exactly what made it out of scope for its own PR: `ctx.speech` already owns the STT/TTS Service Definition, so a competing registry duplicates it rather than completing it. `dsh-speech-agent` is a Consumer of the existing seam, not a second seam.

**Route the transcript through the full agent-loop turn/step machinery (`user/message`/`assistant/message`).** A voice conversation's STT/TTS round trip does not fit the tool-call-oriented step model, and every existing `SessionEventMap` durable-turn precedent (`turn/start`, `step/start`) is owned by `dsh-agent-loop`, not by a capability Consumer. Dedicated `speech-agent/*` log-only events keep the model-visible transcript and response durable (satisfying "model-visible means logged") without taking over the loop's own turn accounting.

**Correlate `speech-agent/turn-interrupted`/`turn-completed` to their generation by matching `textSpoken`/`textRemaining` text against a pending `speech-agent/response`.** An early draft of the invariant did this; it is fuzzy (a coincidental text match would join the wrong generation) and leaks unbounded pending state for sessions with only natural completions. Passing the real `generation` number through `onTurnCompleted`/`onSpeechInterrupted` (sourced from `TurnController.speakingGeneration`) makes the join exact and lets the invariant evict resolved entries deterministically.

## Consequences

A caller gets barge-in cancellation, stale-audio suppression, and Flux-protocol-correct playback offsets "for free" by wiring `TurnController` to already-open sessions; the tradeoff is that this package still does no audio I/O, no eager/speculative generation on Flux STT's `eager-completed`/`resumed` events, and no reconnect — all recorded in the package README's Known Limitations rather than solved here. `examples/voice-agent` demonstrates the full composition: `tests/keyless-smoke.e2e.ts` boots the real `cordis.yml` through the real Loader with fake STT/TTS providers and a scripted `respond()`, and `tests/live.e2e.ts` (opt-in, both `$DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` and `$DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`) drives one turn through a real `ctx.llm.stream()` call and a real Deepgram Flux `/v2/speak` session, printing the demo's latency methodology (`firstAudioLatencyMs`, `turnLatencyMs`) without asserting a fixed ceiling.

## Testing

`packages/speech/speech-agent/tests/turn-controller.spec.ts` covers the full state machine against fake sessions: natural completion, barge-in mid-speech, the race between two barge-ins with no intervening `PlaybackClock.advance()` (proving the second `interrupt()` call omits its offset), generation-fallback attribution when no `turn-started` preceded a terminal TTS event, the unrecognized-event `assertNever` path, and both the Error and non-Error branches of every pump's failure normalization — 100% branch coverage. `tests/playback-clock.spec.ts`, `tests/consumer.spec.ts`, and `tests/invariant.spec.ts` cover the clock's monotonic-offset contract, the durable-logging wrapper's exact event shapes, and the invariant's accept/reject cases respectively. `examples/voice-agent/tests/keyless-smoke.e2e.ts` is the required real-composition test: it proves `ctx.speech` registration/dispatch, `TurnController`, and the durable `speech-agent/*` log all work together through the real Loader.
